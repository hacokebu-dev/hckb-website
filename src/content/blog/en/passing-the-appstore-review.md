After registering my app and waiting anxiously, the result finally came in: "Rejected."

When I looked into the details, it was flagged for "Missing Information" needed for the review. On one hand, I was relieved it wasn't a critical bug or a functional issue, but on the other hand, I was baffled. I had already provided a detailed description and a high-quality screen recording taken directly from the device. What more could they possibly need?

I started digging through other developers' experiences. Some suggested that filming the physical device being used (with another camera) works wonders, while others swore that such a method would *never* get approved. Since my direct screen recording had already been rejected, I decided to give physical filming a shot.

Luckily, I happened to have an overhead camera mount—a "top-down" rig—lying around. (See the image below.)

<img src="/assets/asp2.png">

I originally bought this during a brief, ambitious phase when I dreamed of becoming a YouTuber. It had been sitting in a corner gathering dust, but it finally found its true purpose. I filmed myself actually interacting with the app on the phone and submitted it. 

To my surprise, it passed! Emboldened by this, I used the same "physical filming" method for my next app, and it was approved on the first try as well. Of course, I can't say for certain that this is a guaranteed silver bullet for everyone. However, until it stops working, this is going to be my go-to strategy for all future app submissions.
&nbsp;

&nbsp;  

**Summary**
- My initial submission was rejected for "Missing Information," despite providing detailed descriptions and screen recordings.
- I decided to film the physical act of using the app with an external camera and resubmitted.
- The app was approved, and I successfully repeated this method with another app, passing the review on the first try again.
