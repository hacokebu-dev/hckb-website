I’ve been on a relentless shipping marathon, launching 10 mobile apps and 4 web services in rapid succession. After wrapping up every individual project, I always intended to sit down and write a proper project breakdowns to share the behind-the-scenes engineering hurdles and product post-mortems. But laziness got the best of me, and I kept kicking the can down the road. I bargained with myself, saying, *"Once I hit 10 shipped apps, I'll sit down and write everything."* Well, I actually hit that milestone. So, with a heavy sigh, I finally sat down and plowed through the backlog like a student cramming through a mountain of overdue summer break homework.

Sure, documenting it late is infinitely better than leaving it unwritten, but writing project breakdowns long after the fact feels fundamentally compromised. With my memories of the active development phases heavily faded, the insights feel a bit hollow and lacking in substance. 
Then again, looking at the project breakdowns for 〈Breathe Boy〉—which I wrote almost immediately after shipping—it doesn't read all that differently from the rest. Maybe this is just my baseline as a writer right now.

Moving forward, I want to elevate my writing beyond superficial dev logs and personal diaries. My goal is to structure future essays to deliver concrete, actionable value to the people reading them.

Now that the backlog of project breakdowns is officially cleared, it's time to tackle the overdue project documentation files. (Sigh)
