I had several completed apps ready to go, but I kept putting off the official registration. The reasons were simple: the sheer hassle and the cost.

Since the Apple Developer Program requires an annual fee, I wanted to have a solid portfolio of apps ready before hitting that "Register" button. My initial goal was five, but even after passing that mark, I hesitated. The bureaucracy felt daunting. Finally, I decided to stop stalling and just go for it.

I thought I could just fill out the forms, pay the fee, and be done with it. But the very first hurdle turned out to be a brick wall.

<blockquote>
This Apple Account cannot be used to register through the Apple Developer app. Please visit https://developer.apple.com/kr/programs/enroll/ to continue.
</blockquote>
&nbsp;

I started questioning what I had done wrong. It felt like my account had been blacklisted for no reason.

After some frantic searching, I found various theories. My best guess was that I had included South Korea’s country code (+82) in the contact field, which sometimes causes glitches in the automated system. With no clear fix in sight, I reached out to Apple Support. While waiting, I kept digging and found a common (and discouraging) piece of advice: "Just give up and create a new account." Some even suggested that being part of an "Apple Family Sharing" group could trigger this error. Since my account was linked to a family plan, I wondered if I was doomed from the start.

The next day, I received a surprisingly quick response from Apple. However, the content was confusing. They claimed I had used a different account in the past and told me to use that one instead. But I had never registered for the Developer Program before!

In the end, I took the popular advice and just signed up with a fresh account. This time, everything went smoothly right up to the payment. The fee was 129,000 KRW—roughly $100 USD. Interestingly, Apple’s internal exchange rate felt a bit steeper compared to Google, who usually sticks closer to the real-time market rate.
