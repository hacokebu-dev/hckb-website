To get my apps onto Android, I set out to register for a Google Play Developer account.

While it wasn't quite as bewildering as the Apple Developer Program experience, it certainly wasn't a walk in the park either. I got rejected right at the identity verification stage.
&nbsp;

<img src="/assets/gpb.png">

The system asked for something like a utility bill for verification. I dug through my emails, found a digital water bill, and submitted a screenshot—only to have it promptly rejected. I followed the instructions, so the "failed" notification was frustrating. 

After doing some research, I found that for developers in South Korea, the **"Resident Registration Certificate" (Jumin-deungnok-deungbon)** is the gold standard for verification. Some suggested that you *must* take a physical photo of a printed copy, but I decided to try my luck with a digital screenshot of the certificate. Fortunately, it passed.

Google Play’s developer registration fee is a one-time payment of $25. Compared to Apple, which charges about $100 every single year, Google feels incredibly generous. However, the exchange rate was merciless. That $25 ended up costing me 38,126 KRW.

And with that, I am officially registered on both the Apple App Store and Google Play.

The total cost to "become a developer" (at least on paper) came out to 167,126 KRW.
